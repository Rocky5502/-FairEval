\begin{abstract}
A recommendation that changes after identity or personality context is introduced is not automatically unfair. The change may reflect personalization, harmless sensitivity, or a harmful disparity. FairEval audits this distinction using held-out recommendation utility, matched context interventions, explicit invalid-output accounting, and repeated generations. We evaluate a complete 2,880-generation FairSynth-360 matrix over 120 users and two frozen open-weight models. Identity is relevance-invariant by construction; its nDCG@10 effects are small, with 95\% intervals spanning zero for Phi-3.5-mini ($+0.009$) and Qwen2.5-7B ($-0.008$). True-versus-shuffled synthetic OCEAN likewise provides no reliable utility gain, although user-level effects are heterogeneous. Reliability differs more sharply: Phi is semantically valid on 100\% of cells and achieves mean two-repetition RBO@10 of 0.955, whereas Qwen is valid on 95.2\% and reaches 0.887, with 69 persistent failures retained rather than repaired. A six-family hosted pilot stops outcome-blind after 99/1,080 planned cells and is reported only as operational feasibility. FairEval therefore separates ranking sensitivity, utility consequence, and execution reliability---three quantities that conventional fairness audits can otherwise conflate.
\keywords{LLM recommendation \and fairness \and personalization \and personality \and counterfactual evaluation \and robustness}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models increasingly act as conversational recommenders, where identity and personality context can alter rankings. The central fairness problem is interpretive: a ranking change can reflect useful personalization, harmless sensitivity, or a harmful disparity. Existing LLM-recommendation audits expose attribute sensitivity and stereotyping, but recent work also shows that genuine preferences and cue representation can change the same surface signal \cite{zhang2023fairllm,deldjoo2025cfairllm,kantharuban2025stereotype,neplenbroek2025reading,lutz2025persona,weeber2026cues}. Fairness evaluation therefore needs consequence evidence, not list change alone.

FairEval operationalizes this distinction with candidate-constrained tasks, held-out relevance, matched context interventions, explicit invalid-output accounting, and repeated generations. Personality is treated separately from demographics: the completed FairSynth study uses synthetic OCEAN only as a controlled true-versus-shuffled intervention, while claims about human personality are reserved for dataset-native measured profiles in the registered real-world extension \cite{nguyen2018personality,klec2023bigfive,jiang2024personallm,li2025big5chat}. Prompt structure and candidate order are frozen rather than tuned after seeing outcomes.

This submission asks:
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1:] \textbf{Controlled identity sanity.} Does an identity label known to be irrelevant to relevance cause a reliable held-out utility shift?
\item[RQ2:] \textbf{Controlled personality value.} Does true synthetic OCEAN add utility beyond a matched shuffled-profile control?
\item[RQ3:] \textbf{Execution reliability and black-box feasibility.} How reliably do frozen local models satisfy the ranking contract, and what operational coverage is achieved by a six-family hosted pilot?
\end{description}

The contributions are: (i) a consequence-aware distinction between ranking sensitivity and harm; (ii) a complete 2,880-generation FairSynth evaluation with paired user-level inference; (iii) an auditable output-validity/repeat-stability analysis that exposes different local-model failure modes; and (iv) an outcome-blind six-family hosted feasibility pilot with explicit coverage and cost accounting. Real-world demographic/personality and mitigation extensions remain preregistered but are not presented as completed claims.

\section{Related Work}
Fairness work on LLM recommenders has shown that protected or profile attributes can change rankings and expose stereotypes, but ranking change alone does not establish harm. FaiRLLM and FairEvalLLM develop counterfactual auditing setups; CFaiRLLM explicitly argues for preference-aware interpretation; and recent chatbot studies show that explicit or implicit identity cues can affect both relevance and stereotyping \cite{zhang2023fairllm,deldjoo2024fairevalllm,deldjoo2025cfairllm,kantharuban2025stereotype,neplenbroek2025reading}. FairEval builds on this line by conditioning interpretation on held-out utility and by retaining invalid output as part of recommender behavior rather than silently discarding it.

Prompt and persona construction are themselves evaluation variables. Multi-prompt work and recent persona/cue studies show that conclusions can change under wording or representation choices \cite{mizrahi2024multiprompt,lutz2025persona,weeber2026cues,hua2025promptsensitivity}. Our completed study therefore freezes prompt structure and candidate order across matched contexts; broader cue/paraphrase robustness remains a registered extension.

Personality-aware recommendation predates LLMs and commonly relies on measured Big Five instruments \cite{nguyen2018personality,klec2023bigfive,chen2023reasoner}. LLM-personality work further distinguishes prompted persona expression from externally grounded personality information \cite{jiang2024personallm,sorokovikova2024big5,li2025big5chat,yang2026personae2}. Accordingly, this submission treats FairSynth OCEAN only as a synthetic control and reserves claims about human psychometrics for future dataset-native measured profiles.

\section{Problem Formulation}
For user $u$, let $H_u$ be frozen preference history, $C_u$ an auditable candidate set, $Y_u\subseteq C_u$ held-out relevant items, and $z$ a context condition. A stochastic recommender produces $R_u^{z,s}\sim p_M(R\mid H_u,C_u,z,s)$, where $s$ indexes the frozen repetition/seed. Recommendation utility is $U(R,Y)$ (nDCG@10 and Recall@10). Ranking similarity (RBO/Jaccard) is treated as a sensitivity diagnostic, not as fairness by itself.

For synthetic identity, we compare the observed label with matched alternative labels while holding $H_u$, $C_u$, prompt structure, order, and generation policy fixed. The per-user utility effect is
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_u^{\mathrm{id}}=U(R_u^{\mathrm{obs}},Y_u)-\frac{1}{|A_u|}\sum_{a\in A_u}U(R_u^{a},Y_u),
\end{equation}
where $A_u$ contains the alternative meaningless identity labels. For synthetic personality,
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_u^{\mathrm{pers}}=U(R_u^{p_u},Y_u)-U(R_u^{\pi(p)_u},Y_u),
\end{equation}
with true synthetic OCEAN $p_u$ and a frozen shuffled profile $\pi(p)_u$. Persistent semantic failure is not repaired away: it receives zero end-to-end utility and remains visible in invalid-output rate. This separates preference-conditioned consequence from mere ranking sensitivity.

\section{Benchmark and Evaluation Design}
\subsection{FairSynth Task and Controlled Interventions}
FairSynth-360 is a project-generated stress test with 360 users and 120 items. Each user has eight history items, 30 frozen candidates, five held-out relevant items, a balanced meaningless A/B/C identity label independent of relevance, and a synthetic OCEAN-like vector partially informative about latent preference. The final local matrix uses 120 deterministic users after excluding every protocol-canary user. These are synthetic controls, not observations of human demographics or psychometrics.

Each model returns exactly $K=10$ unique selections from the same candidate set. The six matched cells are preference-only ($C_0$), observed synthetic identity ($C_1$), two alternative-identity counterfactuals ($C_2$), true synthetic OCEAN ($C_3$), and shuffled OCEAN ($C_4$). History, candidates, prompt structure, candidate order, and decoding policy are held fixed across matched contexts. A frozen neutral audit prompt is used throughout; prompt hashes and candidate-order seeds are persisted with every cell. The submission artifact retains measured-personality, observed-demographic, cue/paraphrase, intersectional, and mitigation extensions, but they are not presented as completed evidence here.

\subsection{Models, Compute, and Hosted Budget}
The local stratum freezes Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \cite{qwen25tech} at revision \texttt{a09a3545...} and Phi-3.5-mini-instruct \cite{phi3tech} at \texttt{2fe19245...}. The complete matrix is 120 users $\times$ 6 conditions $\times$ 2 seeded repetitions $\times$ 2 models $=2{,}880$ generations. Models run one at a time on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX~5070~Ti (16~GB) with NF4 4-bit weights, bfloat16 compute, eager attention, and KV caching under Python~3.11.9, PyTorch~2.11.0+cu128, Transformers~4.44.2, Accelerate~0.34.2, and bitsandbytes~0.50.2. Local ranking uses $T=0.2$, top-$p=1.0$, a 512-token cap, and explicit seeds.

The hosted operational panel freezes one gateway configuration from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek, Qwen, and Meta Llama through the audited Zhizengzeng OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Exact-ID preflight blocks silent substitution. The project-wide policy uses a 200 RMB normal stop target, a 250 RMB client-side emergency threshold, and a 2 RMB reserve; the lean FairSynth pilot applies stricter 65/75 RMB runtime thresholds. Balance is reconciled around every persisted request, execution order is immutable, and the 250 RMB threshold is not represented as a provider-enforced atomic spending cap.

\subsection{Metrics and Inference}
Primary utility metrics are nDCG@10 and Recall@10; MRR is a sensitivity metric. Identity sanity compares the observed label with the within-user mean over its alternatives, while personality value compares true with shuffled synthetic OCEAN. Persistent semantic failure receives zero end-to-end utility and contributes to invalid-output rate. User is the inference unit: repetitions are averaged within user-condition, followed by paired bootstrap confidence intervals, paired sign-flip permutation tests, Wilcoxon sensitivity, matched rank-biserial effect size, and Holm correction within the predeclared synthetic contrast families. RBO/Jaccard and the secondary profiles are descriptive only.

\subsection{Output Validation and Protocol Freeze}
Strict serialization and semantic ranking validity are audited separately. A valid ranking contains exactly $K$ unique candidate selections; the raw first response is retained and hashed. Exactly one model generation is allowed per cell. Deterministic normalization may remove only a recognized envelope (whitespace, one JSON fence, or one unambiguous wrapper) and may never alter IDs, order, or length; ambiguous outputs remain invalid.

Earlier sealed canaries exposed output-interface failures and were never scaled. V7 replaces raw output item IDs with deterministic candidate handles (e.g., \texttt{C01}--\texttt{C30}) that map bijectively back to the frozen candidate IDs. After a disjoint V7 canary passed the unchanged 95\% per-model semantic-validity gate, the 2,880-cell matrix was sealed. Final auditing verifies plan linkage, handle mapping, parser provenance, commit/model revisions, and hardware/provider metadata before any paper-facing analysis. Full V4--V7 forensic history remains in the anonymized submission artifact rather than being compressed into the empirical claims.

\subsection{RQ1: Irrelevant Identity Produces No Stable Utility Shift}
Because FairSynth identity is relevance-invariant by construction, a stable utility shift would indicate undesirable context sensitivity. Under preference-only context, mean nDCG@10 is 0.304 for Phi and 0.321 for Qwen (Recall@10: 0.384 and 0.415, respectively), providing the absolute utility scale for the paired effects. Neither model shows a directional identity effect: all identity nDCG@10 and Recall@10 bootstrap intervals include zero after Holm correction (Table~\ref{tab:fairsynth-local-generated}).

\subsection{RQ2: Synthetic Personality Provides No Reliable Incremental Utility}
True-versus-shuffled synthetic OCEAN likewise yields small, uncertain nDCG@10 and Recall@10 effects in both models (Table~\ref{tab:fairsynth-local-generated}). The descriptive per-user distributions add nuance: all four identity/personality $\Delta$nDCG@10 distributions have median zero, but effects are heterogeneous rather than universally absent. For Qwen, personality effects are positive for 43.3\% and negative for 40.0\% of users (IQR approximately $[-0.062,0.079]$); for Phi, 60.8\% of personality contrasts are exactly zero.

\subsection{RQ3: Reliability and Black-Box Feasibility}
Output reliability differs sharply across the two local models. Phi is semantically valid on all 1,440 cells despite requiring deterministic envelope normalization on 1,439 responses; Qwen is strict-JSON compliant on 1,431/1,440 cells but retains 69 semantic failures (54 duplicate selections, six wrong-$K$, five invalid JSON, four non-list payloads), leaving 95.21\% semantic validity. Descriptively, Qwen's invalid rate ranges from 3.75\% under preference-only context to 5.42\% under counterfactual-identity and true-OCEAN contexts, whereas Phi remains at 0\% across all condition families. These failures are never regenerated or repaired.

Two-repetition stability shows the same ordering. Phi has 720/720 valid repeat pairs with mean RBO@10 $=0.955$, Jaccard@10 $=0.863$, and 61.9\% exact-order agreement; Qwen has 673/720 valid pairs with RBO $=0.887$, Jaccard $=0.783$, and 49.3\% exact agreement. Raw generation scores are weakly associated with nDCG (Spearman $\rho=-0.158$ for Phi and $-0.037$ for Qwen), so they are not treated as calibrated confidence.

\section{Theory of Change, Limitations, and Ethical Scope}
\textbf{Theory of change.} The societal problem is that identity-conditioned ranking changes can be misread either as harmless personalization or as bias without checking consequences. FairEval's intended mechanism is: (1) hold preferences/candidates fixed, (2) intervene on context, (3) measure held-out utility rather than list change alone, and (4) expose execution failure instead of repairing it away. If deployed in real audits, this can help practitioners identify preference-irrelevant harms before using stronger mitigation. The necessary preconditions are auditable relevance labels, defensible context variables, and matched candidate tasks; the current FairSynth study validates the mechanism under controlled ground truth, not real-world discrimination.

\textbf{Limits and externalities.} FairSynth cannot establish fairness for human demographic groups or measured psychometrics, and candidate-constrained ranking does not cover unrestricted conversation. The hosted pilot is operational only because it stopped at 99/1,080 cells. Psychographic personalization can also enable intrusive profiling or manipulation; FairEval does not advocate collecting personality by default and never infers missing protected/personality attributes. Counterfactual identity edits are benchmark interventions, not claims that identities are socially interchangeable.

\textbf{Evaluation lesson.} Near-zero mean effects do not imply universal invariance: user-level effects are mixed, and repeat stability differs substantially across models. Likewise, strict JSON compliance is not semantic validity. Fairness audits should therefore report paired consequence distributions, invalid-output behavior, and repeat consistency together rather than treating a clean format or a small average gap as sufficient evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
FairEval evaluates whether identity/personality context changes recommendation utility, not merely whether rankings move. In 2,880 frozen FairSynth generations, irrelevant identity and true-versus-shuffled synthetic OCEAN produce no reliable directional utility effect, while user-level effects remain heterogeneous. Reliability differs more clearly: Phi is fully semantically valid and more repeat-stable than Qwen, whose 69 unrepaired semantic failures remain part of end-to-end behavior. A budget-truncated six-family hosted pilot is reported only as operational evidence. These results support consequence-aware, failure-aware auditing while leaving real-world demographic/personality and mitigation claims to explicitly registered extensions.
